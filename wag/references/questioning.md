# Intake Questioning Methodology

## Purpose

Capture the user's vision without premature specificity. The goal is understanding, not decisions. Decisions come after research.

## Principles

1. **Open-ended first.** Start with "what" and "why" — not "how" or "which."
2. **No premature specificity.** Don't ask about tech stack, scope boundaries, or implementation details during intake. Those answers come after research (Phase B).
3. **Follow the energy.** If the user is excited about a particular aspect, explore it. Don't force a rigid question sequence.
4. **Capture constraints naturally.** Constraints surface through conversation ("we need this by March", "it has to work offline") — don't interrogate for them.
5. **Stop when the vision is clear.** You don't need every detail. You need enough to know what to research.

## Opening

Don't start with a list of questions. Start with one:

> "Tell me what you want to build."

Then listen. Follow up on what they say, not what's on your checklist.

## Areas to explore (not a script)

These are areas to cover through natural conversation, not a sequential questionnaire:

- **The idea** — What is it? What does it do?
- **The motivation** — Why build this? What problem does it solve?
- **The users** — Who uses it? What's their context?
- **The outcome** — What does success look like? How would you know it's working?
- **The context** — Is there existing work? Prior attempts? Related systems?
- **The constraints** — Timeline, budget, team, compliance, platforms — but only what they volunteer or what's clearly relevant.

## Questions to avoid during intake

These force decisions before you have information:

- "What's your tech stack?" — research will inform this
- "What's out of scope?" — scope comes from requirements, after research
- "What's the MVP?" — prioritisation comes after you know what's possible
- "What's the database?" — implementation detail, too early
- "What about auth/payments/deployment?" — these are solution-space questions, not problem-space

## Output

A VISION.md document that captures:
- What they want to build and why
- Who it's for
- What success looks like
- Any constraints that surfaced naturally

This is NOT a PRD. It's a seed. The PRD comes after research and requirements.
